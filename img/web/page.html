What is this?

PocketPix is a text-to-picture model made to fit on a Game Boy Advance cartridge and run on its 16.8 MHz processor. The first-pass model has about 2 million weights; it lays out a 16×16 picture one pixel at a time. Two enlarger models (about 10 million weights each) then repaint it at 32×32 and at 64×64. All the models together take about 25 MB of the cartridge. Every pixel is one of 256 fixed colours.

What to expect: small, blurry, impressionistic pictures. It is best with one thing and a colour, such as car red or flower yellow; it is poor at counting, at text, at faces in detail and at anything needing several things arranged in a particular way. It knows about four thousand words and ignores the rest. Without a prompt it paints whatever it likes.

This page runs the cartridge's own picture engine, the same C code with the same models and settings, compiled for the browser, so it works in whole numbers exactly as the cartridge does. Here a picture takes well under a second; on the cartridge it takes , . Nothing you type leaves your browser.

The models were trained on CC BY photographs from CommonCatalog (the CC-BY subset, from the CommonCanvas project) and on close-up crops from Open Images V7 (photographs CC BY 2.0, object boxes CC BY 4.0).
